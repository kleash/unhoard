Delete similar photos on iPhone

Exact copies and near-identical re-takes need different tools. Here's how to handle both.

1. Exact duplicates: the Duplicates album

  1. Open Photos and scroll to Utilities › Duplicates.
  2. Each row is a set of copies of the same picture. Tap Merge to keep one.

The extra copies go to Recently Deleted. If you don't see the album, Photos hasn't found any duplicates (or is still looking).

2. Bursts and re-takes: select them by hand

  • Bursts: Photos › Media Types › Bursts. Open one, tap Select, pick the frames to keep, tap Done and choose to keep only those.
  • Re-takes: in your Library, tap Select, tap the shots you don't want (drag across a row to select several), then tap the bin. Pinch to zoom in and compare first.

3. Unhoard's Similar Shots

Unhoard groups photos that are near-identical rather than exact copies: shots taken within two minutes of each other that look alike, like five tries at the same group photo.

  • In each group, one shot is marked Best: a favourite if there is one, then the one with the best faces, then the sharpest.
  • The rest are put in the delete tray as a suggestion. Favourites, edited photos and photos in your albums are never put there for you.
  • Compare shows a group two at a time, zooming both together. Tap a shot to keep it instead of the best pick, or tap an item in the tray to keep it.
  • Nothing is deleted until you tap Delete and confirm in the iOS dialog. Deleted photos then wait in Recently Deleted for 30 days.

The grouping isn't perfect: two different poses at the same spot can land in one group, so look through each group before you delete. It works on your iPhone, and exact duplicates are left to the Duplicates album above.

Get the space back

Deleted photos keep using space until Recently Deleted is emptied: Photos › Utilities › Recently Deleted › Select › Delete All. If you use an iCloud Shared Library, remember that deleting a shared photo removes it for everyone in it.
